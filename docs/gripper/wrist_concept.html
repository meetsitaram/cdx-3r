Gripper: a wrist that moves

Concept for review before modelling. CDX-3R gripper, 19 Oct 2026.

The grip is bolted rigidly to the forearm frame now, so holding it locks your wrist. The plan puts a passive three-axis joint between the forearm frame and a hand frame that carries the grip, trigger, rail and claws. Every joint axis passes through the centre of your wrist, so the handle follows your hand instead of fighting it.

The joint chain

  1. Turn (palm up / down, about ±70°): a 6816 bearing (80 mm bore, 100 mm outside, 10 mm thick) in a housing on the wrist ring's front face. Your forearm passes through it as it passes through the ring today.
  2. Bend (wrist up / down, about ±50°): two pivots on the thumb and little-finger sides of your wrist, on one line through the wrist centre.
  3. Side to side (about ±20°): one pivot on the back of the wrist; its axis points into the wrist, through the same centre.

What changes

  • The rail and claws move onto the hand frame over the back of your hand. Stowed, the claws sit over the back of the hand and fingers, raised clear of them; slid out, they reach past your fingertips. The rail gets shorter.
  • Grip, trigger, cable and pusher are all on the hand frame, so the cable no longer crosses a joint.
  • The wrist cuff that closes round the wrist goes; the bearing housing takes its place on the ring, and hand straps keep the hand on the grip (your earlier idea).
  • New parts to buy: one 6816-2RS bearing; the pivots reuse 608 bearings and M8 bolts like the shoulder.

Assumptions to check at a fitting

  • Wrist joint centre about 15 mm past the wrist ring's front face (the scan has no hand). The pivots can be moved later.
  • Hand about 85 mm wide and 30 mm thick at the knuckles.
